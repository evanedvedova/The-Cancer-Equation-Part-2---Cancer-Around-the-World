# Series: The-Cancer-Equation
# Part-2---Cancer-Around-the-World
In Part 2 of The Cancer Equation series, we shift our focus from cancer research to the disease itself. We explore the global burden of cancer, identify the most common cancer types, and examine differences between incidence and mortality.

An important question drives this analysis: Does the global research effort reflect the actual burden of cancer?

By comparing cancer incidence and mortality with clinical trial activity analysed in Part 1, we aim to explore whether the cancers receiving the most research attention are also those affecting the largest number of people.

## 1. Project Overview

The previous part identified most common cancer types studied in clinical trials. 

But how does this research landscape compare with the actual burden of cancer?

Which cancer types affect the largest number of people? Are the most frequently diagnosed cancers also responsible for the highest number of deaths? And does the distribution of clinical research reflect these patterns?

This project aims to answer these questions through three main perspectives:

 -Cancer incidence: Which cancer types account for the highest number of new cases worldwide?
 -Cancer mortality: Which cancer types cause the highest number of deaths?
- Research activity: How does the distribution of clinical trials compare with the global cancer burden?

The ultimate goal is to put clinical research into a broader context and explore the relationship between the burden of disease and the research efforts directed towards it.

## 4. Key Findings & Results

First, let's have a look on cancer types around the world in Figure 1. The graph shows incidence and mortality of top 15 cancer types. 

<img width="4880" height="3416" alt="graphic-absolute-numbers-inc-and-mort-both-sexes-in-2024-continents" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/cf156bab-c72b-4fa2-b315-c6c880f4b2bc" />
Figure 1: Incidence and mortality for top 15 cancer sites in 2024, absolute numbers

Lung cancer ranks first in both incidence and mortality, with approximately 2.5 million new cases and 1.8 million deaths. 
It therefore represents a particularly significant global health challenge, with a substantial number of deaths relative to new diagnoses.

Breast cancer ranks second in incidence, with approximately 2.3 million new cases. However, its mortality is considerably lower, 
at around 670,000 deaths. This illustrates that the cancer types with the highest incidence do not necessarily have the highest mortality.

Colorectal cancer ranks third in both incidence and mortality, highlighting its considerable contribution to the global cancer burden.

Prostate cancer also stands out on fourth position, ranking among the most frequently diagnosed cancers, while its mortality is considerably lower than its incidence.

Secondly, let's use the data from part 1 to examine where clinical research activity is concentrated. 
To make data comparable, I am using the pbi file from part 1, using a slicer to zoom into the same year 2024.

Breast cancer dominates the clinical trial landscape, with approximately 960 studies, making it the most frequently studied cancer type in this selection.

Prostate cancer follows with approximately 440 studies, while colorectal cancer ranks third with around 370 studies.

Lung cancer and non-small cell lung cancer also feature prominently, followed by ovarian cancer.

The distribution suggests that breast cancer receives particularly high research attention, while prostate and colorectal cancer also represent major areas of clinical investigation.
